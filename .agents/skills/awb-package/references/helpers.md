# Diagnosing a helper's rows

`awb-init`'s `scripts/install_helpers.py` installs `src/packaging/draft.py` and restores it when missing. Call the project's copy, customized or not; open its source only to adapt or debug it.

- Every draft check reads `findings.md` and `methodology.md` as they render, through one reading in the helper (`_source_lines` and `_view`), so a form that hides text from a reader hides it from every check and nothing a reader sees is skipped. Comments do not render: a line starting `<!--` opens a comment that ends on the line holding `-->`, or at the end of the file; elsewhere a comment must close within its paragraph, or its `<!--` shows as text. Fenced code is code. Emphasis and strikethrough markers, HTML tags, escapes, character references, and zero-width characters are read as the text they render, so `**leads** to` reads as leads to. A heading is a `#` line indented at most three spaces, its text compared as it renders with whitespace collapsed; a **Claim strength:** line counts under the same indentation, since a line four spaces in may be code.
- `findings` rows (`check_findings(path, *, names=(), roots=())`): `line`, `kind` (`code`, `path`, `file`, `identifier`, `sha`, or `name`), and `text`. Comments and Markdown link targets are skipped.
- A `name` row's `text` is a name from these sources: the investigation and package names; the view and table names created or read in `foundation/views/*.sql` and in the views the investigation's evidence cites, without CTE names or table functions; the settings keys of `settings.toml` and of the settings files and resolved settings that the investigation's evidence records, as top-level keys and the dotted path of every nested key (a nested key's bare name is usually a plain word, so it is left out); and the result IDs of `investigations/<investigation>/evidence/*.json` and the evidence links in its brief, state, and history. A name of letters only is never in the set, since it collides with ordinary words. `check-draft` passes those plain-word names as `roots` instead: a slash token with any recorded name as a segment, such as `orders/region`, is a `path` row, while a slash between two other plain words, such as yes/no, is prose.
- `charts` rows (`check_charts(findings_path, charts_dir)`): `chart` and `problem`, one per specification numbered out of order, specification without a file, file without a specification, non-chart file in `charts/`, or file without data rows.
- `headings` rows (`heading_problems(draft)`): `heading` and `problem` for a `###` finding heading that methodology has no section for, or that findings or methodology repeats; `file`, `line`, and `problem` for a heading inside a list item or block quote, a heading indented four or more spaces, or paragraph text underlined with `=` or `-`, which renders as a heading the slide structure does not place.
- `claims` rows (`claim_problems(draft, represented, claims)`): a causal phrase above its slide's claim strength as `line`, `section`, `phrase`, `claim`, `results`, and `problem`; a represented result without a recognized Claim cell as `result` and `problem`; a finding without a methodology section, or whose section's **Claim strength:** lines are missing, repeated, unrecognized, at another strength, or, for a causal claim, naming a design other than the one `state.md` records for its cited results, as `section` and `problem`. Sections sharing a heading pool their citations and declarations. The phrase list is `_CAUSAL` in the helper; whole words only, so data-driven is prose.
- `verify` and `compare_trees` rows: `path`, `kind` (`missing`, `extra`, `size`, `checksum`), `expected`, and `actual`. `compare_trees(local_dir, copy_dir)` compares every file, manifest included, with the local release as expected. Invalid inventories, missing or unreadable directories, and symlinks raise errors, which the commands report under `errors` or `problem`.
