# Diagnosing a helper's rows

`awb-init`'s `scripts/install_helpers.py` installs `src/packaging/draft.py` and restores it when missing. Call the project's copy, customized or not; open its source only to adapt or debug it.

- `findings` rows (`check_findings(path, *, names=(), roots=())`): `line`, `kind` (`code`, `path`, `file`, `identifier`, `sha`, or `name`), and `text`. HTML comments and Markdown link targets are skipped.
- A `name` row's `text` is a name from these sources: the investigation and package names; the view and table names created or read in `foundation/views/*.sql` and in the views the investigation's evidence cites, without CTE names or table functions; the settings keys of `settings.toml` and of the settings files and resolved settings that the investigation's evidence records, as top-level keys and the dotted path of every nested key (a nested key's bare name is usually a plain word, so it is left out); and the result IDs of `investigations/<investigation>/evidence/*.json` and the evidence links in its brief, state, and history. A name of letters only is never in the set, since it collides with ordinary words. `check-draft` passes those plain-word names as `roots` instead: a slash token with any recorded name as a segment, such as `orders/region`, is a `path` row, while a slash between two other plain words, such as yes/no, is prose.
- `charts` rows (`check_charts(findings_path, charts_dir)`): `chart` and `problem`, one per specification numbered out of order, specification without a file, file without a specification, non-chart file in `charts/`, or file without data rows.
- `numbers` rows (`check_numbers(path, values, *, allowed=())`): `file`, `line`, and `text`, the number as written with its unit or the letters written against it, such as `21.3%`, `4.2M`, `1.5e6`, or `5GB`; a currency sign is left out, and so is a USD, EUR, GBP, JPY, CHF, CAD, AUD, CNY, or INR code written against the number, as in `USD4.2M`. The values are each represented result's `results/<id>.csv` cells, the numbers in its `results/<id>.profile.json`, and its evidence's check details and settings, as magnitudes, so a sign carried by words such as "fell" still traces. JSON numbers are read exactly as written, never through a binary float. A cell that is a number whole is that number; any other cell, check detail, or settings text is read the way a narrative line is, adding each number it states and, with a scale, the scaled value too, so `$4.2M` records 4,200,000 and an ID such as `ORD-123` records nothing.
  - **Tracing.** A number written to d decimal places traces to a value within half a unit of its last place (`1.5e6` to within 50,000), compared exactly at every written digit; a percent or percentage points also to a fraction 100 times smaller (`21%` to 0.214); a scale such as `k`, `M`, `bn`, or `million` also to the scaled value; and the lower bound of a range such as `10-15%` or `10 to 15%` takes the upper bound's unit. `%` and the word units (`percent`, `per cent`, `percentage points`, `pp`, `thousand`, `million`, `billion`, `trillion`, `mn`, `bn`, `tn`) belong to their number across any whitespace, a line wrap included; a letter scale (`k`, `M`, `b`) or `x` only when written against it, so `5 m` is 5. Scientific notation is read with any mantissa, such as `1e-5` or `12.5e3`; an exponent too large to hold never traces. Digits joined by dots, commas, slashes, or colons, or three-digit groups joined by spaces, are one number: thousands grouping such as `1,240` and a single decimal point trace; a version or address, three or more dotted parts not all three-digit groups such as `3.12.1` or `10.0.0.1`, is skipped; and any other join, such as `1,2,3`, the decimal comma `21,3%`, `3/4`, `14:05`, `1.234.567`, or `1 000 000`, is reported and never traces, so write it as `1, 2, 3`, `21.3%`, `75%`, or `1,234,567`.
  - **Reading fails safe.** A number is skipped only in one of the contexts below, each of which marks it as no quantity. Every other number is read, including one whose context leaves it in doubt, and is reported unless it traces or is allowed; clear such a row by rewording, such as ending a date or year with a comma, or with an `allowed_numbers` entry and its reason. A line that wraps a paragraph, in a quote too, is read with the lines around it, so a context or a unit split from its number by the wrap is still seen. Where a context below says the period ends, it ends at punctuation, at a dash before a space, or at the end of its paragraph: a word after it could make its last number a count, as in `in 2000 orders`.
  - **Skipped.** Code, HTML comments, and link targets. Paths whole: a slash token, starting with a letter, `_`, `~`, `./`, or `../`, that starts with `./`, `../`, or `~`, has two or more slashes, starts with a project folder (`data`, `foundation`, `investigations`, `deliveries`, `src`, `views`, `queries`, `results`, `evidence`, `exploration`, `scans`), or ends in a file name with a known extension, such as `results/r-001-77.csv`. A file name with a known extension (`.md`, `.py`, `.csv`, `.tsv`, `.parquet`, `.json`, `.jsonl`, `.sql`, `.png`, `.svg`, `.jpg`, `.jpeg`, `.pdf`, `.htm`, `.html`, `.duckdb`, `.db`, `.xls`, `.xlsx`, `.docx`, `.pptx`, `.yaml`, `.yml`, `.toml`, `.txt`, `.log`, `.ipynb`). A hyphenated identifier whose first part is one letter, holds a digit or `_`, or has a capital after its first letter, such as `ORD-123-45`, `COVID-19`, `Q-3`, or `r-001`, and the chart file stem `chart-1`. A number glued to a letter before it or to a name's `_` (`Q2`, `FY2026`, `H1`, `late_77`), or to letters and digits after it (a hash such as `3f9a2c1`); a version or address, as above; a zero-padded code such as release `002`. Numeric dates (`2026-06-30`, with or without a time, and `6/30/2026`), and times with am, pm, UTC, or GMT or with seconds (`9:30am`, `14:05:30`). A day from 1 to 31 before or after a month name, with or without a year, where the date ends or runs to another date (`June 5, 2026,`, `5 June.`, `June 1, 2026 to June 30, 2026`). A year from 1900 to 2099 after in, from, since, during, until, till, through, before, after, between, year, FY, CY, fiscal, calendar, early, mid, late, a month name, or a quarter or half such as Q3 or H1, where the period ends (`in 2025.`, `mid-2025.`, `Q3 2025,`). Years listed after such a year as a range (`2023-2024`, `2023 to 2025`, `2023 vs 2024`) or a list that and or or closes (`2019, 2020, and 2021`), where the list ends. The one number after Chart or Charts, and the rest of a Charts list listed the same way where the list ends (`Charts 1 and 4.`). A list marker where a list starts, a heading's leading section number (`## 2. Sources`), and footnote marks.
  - **Read.** So reported unless they trace or are allowed: `In June 77 orders`, `June 5 we met`, both numbers of `on June 5 77 orders`, `On June 5, 2026 we met` (2026), `in 2000 orders`, `Q3 2025 revenue` (2025), `(2025)`, `between 1950 and 2000 stores`, `Charts 1 and 4 show it` (4), `1950 orders`, `over 2025`, `2026 H1`, `Python 3.12`, `Week 23`, `top-10`, `Total,77`, `West/21.3%`, `100ms`, an ordinal such as `95th` (its number traces), and a number other than 1 that starts a line continuing a paragraph (`rose to` then `77. Orders`), which Markdown does not make a list.
  - **Allowed.** An `allowed` text covers a number wherever that text is written around it on the same line: a bare `23` covers every 23 in both narratives, and the phrase `Week 23` only that one.
  - **Limit.** The check proves a number is among the recorded values, not that it is its claim's value or that both narratives give a claim the same number: with a large represented table most small numbers trace. Numbers written as words, such as three, or as a single symbol, such as ½, are not read, so write a claim's numbers in digits. `awb-package`'s **Done** step confirms by hand that every claim in `findings.md`, with its numbers, traces to the matching claim in `methodology.md`.
- `verify` and `compare_trees` rows: `path`, `kind` (`missing`, `extra`, `size`, `checksum`), `expected`, and `actual`. `compare_trees(local_dir, copy_dir)` compares every file, manifest included, with the local release as expected. Invalid inventories, missing or unreadable directories, and symlinks raise errors, which the commands report under `errors` or `problem`.
