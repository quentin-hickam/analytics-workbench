## 2. Ranked pain points

1. **Confident interpretations outran the evidence — major.** In the first results, December's low repeat rate was attributed to probable Christmas buyers, and France and Germany were described as having growth spread across many accounts based on buyer counts. The follow-up showed three accounts supplied 99.6% of Germany's growth. Later, early cohort rates were called definitely overstated without establishing the direction of bias. What would help: distinguish measured results from explanations before presenting findings, and check growth contributions before making claims about breadth.
2. **Too much implementation narration — major.** During data preparation and drafting I saw settings-file errors, shell splitting problems, validation changes, repeated full-review passes and commit details. “Now the friction log” was particularly unexplained. What would help: concise updates about commercial findings, uncertainty and the next review point; fix routine coding problems without narrating each one.
3. **Audience methods included internal validation thresholds — major.** The draft's “How we know” slide and short methodology note listed percentages of rows that filters had to keep. Those thresholds did not tell leadership why the answers were reliable. What would help: business-language checks from the first draft. My request for reconciliation wording led to actual recorded reconciliation checks, which was valuable, but I expected basic totals to have been reconciled already.
4. **Initial answers were summaries rather than complete answers — minor.** The first results omitted the full 24 monthly figures and 12 cohort counts and rates. “124 cancellations” also needed clarification as invoices versus lines. What would help: provide a compact supporting table or link with each answer, including denominators and units.
5. **Ambiguous cleaning recommendation — minor, with potentially major consequences.** The agent recommended deleting repeated lines within invoices because they looked double-scanned. I could not establish that from this export. What would help: retain ambiguous records by default, distinguish them clearly from the proven cross-sheet copy, and give a sensitivity estimate. The agent did ask before deleting them, so this did not become a blocker.
6. **Extra approval stages and precision changes — minor.** After accepting findings I still had to request the headline preview; the full draft then replaced rounded headline numbers with overly precise values that I asked to round again. What would help: combine acceptance with the next concrete preview when possible and keep approved presentation precision unless a correction changes the message.
7. **Caveat tracking was inconsistent — minor.** Status said only the repeated-line caveat remained, overlooking missing IDs, first-observed customer history and other limits. What would help: distinguish “no unanswered questions” from “no limitations,” and report the full material caveat set consistently.

## 3. README

The first-message example was useful. It prompted me to give the questions, decision, audience, length, source, storage locations and headline preference together. The explanation of package contents, frozen releases and the PowerPoint handoff was clear.

The README is long for a busy first-time user. I mainly needed the first-message example, the usual sequence and an example of the review/release handoff. The installation and internal workflow material makes it harder to scan, even though it is labeled for administrators.

I would have benefited from clearer expectations that:

- Keeping all five findings and an implications slide could push an eight-slide request to nine main slides, with methods and limitations extra.
- Choosing supporting tables would add a required supporting-datasets appendix slide. I learned this after approving the headline count.
- A “checked” finding can still contain an unsupported interpretation; the README mentions limits to checks, but a concrete example would make that warning easier to act on.
- Basic result checks and reconciliation of totals are different. I did not know I would need to ask for totals to reconcile before that check was recorded.

I found no clear false promise about the output: it explicitly says it does not draw slides. The quoted exchange ranges should remain a guide; I cannot assess elapsed time from this exchange alone.

## 4. Agent communication and scoping

The initial scoping questions were mostly good: recommendations, alternatives and concrete business definitions. Booked-month cancellations, later-day repeat orders, meaningful product sales and growth in pounds were sensible decisions to surface. Supplying storage locations once worked; I was not asked again.

The fourth question bundled several different thresholds. “Full two years” also left the incomplete December 2011 boundary ambiguous, so I specified the same complete 24 months across analyses. The proposed country cutoff risked hiding small emerging markets; keeping them separately was better.

The final commercial language was much better than the operational updates. “Largest buyers” versus “largest contributors to revenue growth” mattered: those are different groups. Pooling anonymous orders as “one buyer” was also misleading until changed to “unidentified-orders group.” Terms such as provenance, producer and filter thresholds were unnecessary for me. The agent's long replies often mixed useful findings with details I could not use.

## 5. Trust in the answers

I accepted all five quantitative answers by the end. The monthly table, exact year-on-year change, cohort numerators and denominators, and product sales/cancelled values were concrete enough to assess. Explaining both customer concentration denominators was particularly helpful: 54.0% of total revenue and 63.1% of identified-customer revenue answer different questions.

I trusted the overseas conclusions more after the contribution analysis corrected the Germany claim and confirmed the headline contributors were identified accounts. I accepted the reported overlap correction because it was described as a line-by-line match, distinct from ordinary repeated entries. I did not independently inspect the workbook or recompute the results.

I did not trust the initial Christmas explanation, the claim that buyer counts established broad growth, or the assertion that early cohort rates were definitely overstated. Those were withdrawn or qualified. Repeated clean-review claims and two rounding corrections reminded me that passing checks is useful but does not replace reading the content. I would not infer a marketing budget split, cancellation causes or marketing returns from these data; the final package appropriately frames actions as tests.
