# Feedback from Priya Natarajan

## 1. Outcome and satisfaction

I got the five quantitative answers, a reviewed deck-building package, a readable methodology note and four useful supporting tables. Release 001 reached the agreed releases folder, and I checked that the files and build instructions were there. My satisfaction is **8/10**. I would use this again, particularly for repeatable reporting, but I would still review the commercial claims closely.

This did not establish the optimal marketing budget split. The final recommendation correctly acknowledges that we lack costs, margins and campaign results. It gives leadership a sensible basis for protecting key accounts and designing tests. I still need to build the PowerPoint; the README made that clear from the beginning.

## 2. Ranked pain points

1. **Plausible explanations presented as established facts — major.** At the headline stage, accounting entries were said to affect totals “not product demand”. Later, a period without transactions became a “Christmas closure”, and the agent suggested early cohorts' repeat rates were pushed upwards. I had to separate observations from explanations. It would help to apply that distinction before asking me to approve findings, especially when the proposed action involves spending money.
2. **Transaction rows removed before asking — major.** During ingestion, the agent removed 22,523 identical cross-sheet lines and told me afterwards. I agreed with the correction, but the README said I would decide whether overlapping rows were copies unless the source settled it. No source confirmation was reported. Show the evidence and obtain the business decision before changing uncertain records.
3. **Routine technical repairs became approval gates — minor.** Near release, I was asked whether an internal sentence could cite the correct monthly result instead of the yearly table. That did not change a number or claim. Fix and report such issues without involving me. The earlier one-word headline decision also felt disproportionate, although the README had warned me about that rule.
4. **Answers were declared complete before I could inspect them — minor.** The first results message offered revenue highlights and cohort ranges rather than all 24 monthly revenues and 12 cohort rates. I had to ask for the actual answers to my monthly questions. Supply compact complete tables alongside the executive summary.
5. **The draft changed an agreed presentation choice — minor.** The promised year-by-year reconciliation table became a bar chart dominated by product revenue. I had to request the table again so fees and adjustments were readable. Preserve agreed chart choices or explain a proposed change before drafting.
6. **Review-rule scope did not match my request — minor.** I requested language checks for this investigation. The agent later said it could only apply them globally or to one package and selected global. That works today but could be inappropriate in an unrelated investigation. Explain the limitation when asking the scope question and offer a clear choice.

## 3. README

The opening example, usual requests and explanation of the package contents were helpful. I understood that the workbench would not produce a PowerPoint itself. The release numbering, external storage and START-HERE instructions matched the experience.

The document is long for a first-time commercial user. I would prefer a short analyst guide with installation and administration in a separate document. The terminology section helps, but there is still quite a lot to absorb before starting.

The overlap decision described in the README was not followed as I experienced it. That needs consistent behaviour more than extra documentation. The README also says thresholds are proposed before seeing which rows lead; proposing to adjust the product threshold if the list looked too long or short made that commitment feel uncertain.

I needed clearer practical boundaries on what approval is for: business definitions and material claims should come to me; routine evidence-reference repairs should not. The review section does warn that issues on unchanged text come back to the user, but experiencing this for a citation made the rule feel too broad.

The README describes package-specific or general review rules, but I did not realise that applying one to an investigation was unavailable. The agent should make supported scopes explicit when asking. Clearer upfront counting of main slides, mandatory slides and appendices would also help; “about eight” ultimately became nine main slides plus two appendix slides, which I accepted.

## 4. Communication and scoping

The questions were mostly good: concrete recommendations, alternatives and commercial reasons. Cancellation timing, later-day repeats and keeping ambiguous repeated invoice lines were easy decisions to make from the explanations. Questions arrived in manageable groups.

The initial recommendation to define net revenue as products only was not right for my company-total question. It could have led to a misleading headline if I had simply accepted every recommendation. Offering all-line totals with a product/non-product reconciliation would have been better.

The agent generally used plain language. “Provisional”, “confirmed”, evidence records and review mechanics sometimes took more space than I needed. Replies were well organised but long, with repeated completion summaries. The short progress messages reassured me that work was continuing, although messages about writing settings or fixing a forgotten heading offered little business value. I cannot judge elapsed processing time from the exchange alone.

It proposed another audience question despite my having already supplied the leadership audience and decision. I supplied several remaining definitions together to keep things moving. The December follow-up questions were worthwhile, and the answers were specific.

## 5. Trust in the answers

I accepted all five final answers, based on the reported checks and the files I reviewed; I did not independently recalculate the workbook.

- **Revenue and seasonality:** I trusted the final monthly table and the product/non-product reconciliation. The two-year autumn pattern was clearly quantified. My confidence improved once all months were shown and the accounting entries were visible. The unsolicited overlap removal initially reduced that confidence.
- **Customer concentration:** I trusted the final figures because both denominators were explicit: 63.6% of identified-customer revenue and 55.1% of total revenue, with £2.47m lacking an ID. The initial summary omitted the identified-customer qualifier, which could mislead leadership.
- **Repeat buying:** I accepted the figures after the agent confirmed December coverage and full 90-day follow-up and showed cohort sizes. I would use them as first-observed cohort measures, not true new-customer retention rates. I did not trust the unsupported closure explanation or the suggested direction of early-cohort bias; both were corrected.
- **Product cancellations:** I accepted the ranking with its fixed £2,000 threshold and cancellation-value definition. The single large cancellation behind the leading product materially changes its interpretation. I would investigate before taking product action, rather than assume the rate proves a persistent problem.
- **Overseas growth:** The account-concentration check was especially valuable. Australia's £108k increase could have encouraged the wrong acquisition decision; knowing 97% came from one account changes the discussion. France's growth and customer breadth justify a test candidate, not a guaranteed return.

I did not accept concentration alone as a reason to shift the budget towards retention. The final recommendation to test retention alongside acquisition is appropriate.

## 6. Package review and release

I reviewed the headline preview, the full findings outline, the audience methods note and START-HERE, and sampled the supporting CSVs. The outline's notes and caveats made it possible to review the commercial story without reading code. The methods note was understandable and the supporting data useful.

I requested real corrections: clearer customer language, cautious recommendations, the revenue reconciliation table and removal of wording that implied December was incomplete. Those corrections were incorporated and shown back to me. The independent review reportedly found and repaired problems, which was useful, though it also generated internal approval work I should not have had to do.

Release was straightforward once I authorised it. The agent supplied the full external path, stated the remaining caveats and reported that the copy matched. I checked the release folder and build instructions. The frozen release and separation of audience files from builder files are useful safeguards.

## 7. Wasted turns or effort

The avoidable effort came from requesting complete monthly answers after receiving only highlights, correcting unsupported explanations, restoring the promised reconciliation table, and approving an internal citation repair. Several of these were combined with useful decisions, so I would not call every exchange wasted. The final citation question was the clearest unnecessary gate.

The headline approval checkpoint was useful. Counting individual words and reporting multiple rounds of technical review details was less useful to me. I want to spend my review time on whether leadership will understand and act appropriately on the evidence.

## 8. What worked well

The setup and download required no programming from me. Original data and released files went to the locations I supplied. Definitions were recorded and carried into a readable methodology note. The agent responded well to corrections and produced the complete tables when asked.

The strongest analytical contribution was checking whether overseas growth reflected a broad market or one customer. The revenue reconciliation and cohort-size checks were also commercially useful. The status request gave a clear picture of progress, and the final package had practical instructions for the next person building the slides.

I would return for another investigation, with a standing expectation that uncertain data changes come to me first, claims stay within the evidence, and routine technical repairs happen without a separate permission round.
